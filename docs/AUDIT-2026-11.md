# Site audit: three reviewers, one plan

November 6, 2026. The staging build was reviewed from three seats: the cardiologist, who wants the site to grow the practice; the office manager, who runs the phones and the front desk; and a web designer with 20+ years of practice sites behind them. Their findings were handed to the designer, who turned them into the plan at the end. Items marked **Done** were built in this pass. The rest need input from the practice.

The no-invention rule still governs every change. New copy written for approval is marked **Draft** in staging and is excluded from production until Dr. Abela approves it.

---

## 1. The cardiologist

**Goal:** grow the practice. That means more new patients, and especially more physician referrals, which drive most specialist volume.

**Likes**
- Credentials are visible right under the hero. The four ABIM boards and FACC say "specialist" before anyone scrolls.
- The office-versus-hospital labels on each condition set honest expectations.
- The research page gives credibility with other physicians.
- Phone and directions are one tap away on every screen.

**Doesn't like**
- **Nothing speaks to referring physicians.** A primary care doctor or another cardiologist has no way to find how to send a patient, where to fax, or which hospitals he covers. This is the biggest growth gap.
- **The site doesn't say why to choose him.** His unusual combination of interventional cardiology, advanced heart failure and imaging (echo and CT) is spread across lists. It is never stated as a reason.
- The homepage asks visitors to choose twice, once by symptom and again by condition. It feels long.
- Without a photo it feels anonymous. Patients choose a doctor's face.
- He can't see what the page looks like without the review badges.

**Wants added**
1. A page for referring physicians: how to refer, fax number, what to send, NPI, hospitals, and what happens after the consult.
2. A "Why patients and physicians choose Dr. Abela" section built from facts (training breadth, procedures, research), with no superlatives.
3. Room for patient reviews. These come only from real patients with written consent, or from a link to a public review profile.
4. Visibility for second opinions on procedure recommendations. Phrasing needs his approval.
5. Neighborhoods and areas served, for local search. This needs his list.

## 2. The office manager

**Goal:** fewer phone calls for questions the site could answer, and calls that arrive already sorted.

**Likes**
- Phone number everywhere, no contact form collecting medical details, clear 911 language.
- The "What to bring" list. It shortens check-in.

**Doesn't like**
- **No office hours, parking or building directions.** These are the top three reasons people call. They are still missing from the practice side.
- Every caller looks the same. New patients, current patients, referring offices and records requests all land on one phone number with no guidance.
- No instructions for refills, test results, records requests or after-hours care.
- The fax number is buried on the Contact page. Referring offices need it on the first screen they see.

**Wants added**
1. A "How can we help?" router that sends new patients, current patients, referring offices and records requests each to the right place.
2. A "Current patients" section on Contact: refills, results, records and after-hours, with pending placeholders until the office writes the process.
3. Fax and NPI on the referral page and in the footer.
4. A printable referral page that offices can keep at the front desk.
5. Fields for new-patient forms (PDF), visit length, holiday closures and staff languages.

## 3. The web designer (20+ years)

**Overall:** the craft is solid. Contrast passes, motion is restrained, the type system holds, and production is safe. The issues are **information architecture and growth plumbing**, not polish.

**Findings**
- **IA:** two overlapping choosers on the homepage (symptoms, then conditions). Merge them: symptoms become quick chips inside the Conditions section. That removes a section and a decision.
- **Navigation:** seven items once Referrals is added is too many. Drop "Home" (the logo already does that job) and use "For physicians" as the label.
- **Audience routing:** the office manager's router is the right pattern. Put it mid-page, after conditions, where scanners land.
- **Proof:** the "why" section must be built only from verifiable facts. Reviews need a real source, never invented ones.
- **Sharing:** links pasted into texts, email or LinkedIn show no preview. Add Open Graph and Twitter tags with a branded share image, and an Apple touch icon.
- **Missing basics:** a 404 page.
- **Measurement:** growth is not measurable yet. Tag every call and directions click (`data-event`) so any privacy-friendly analytics tool can count them once the practice picks one.
- **Review workflow:** the cardiologist needs a clean preview. Add a "Hide review markers" switch to the staging banner.
- **Content status:** editorial copy written for approval was labelled "public record", which is misleading. Add a separate **Draft** status so he can see exactly what needs his sign-off.
- **Local SEO (phase 2):** individual pages per condition ("Advanced heart failure care in Tampa") rank better than one long page. Don't ship them until each has about 300 approved words; thin pages hurt more than they help.

## 6. Local engineering audit — November 6, 2026

Checked out the repository on `codex/audit-hardening` and verified the current build, rather than relying on the earlier Lighthouse/axe results above.

### Findings and fixes

| Priority | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| High | Production currently publishes only Home and Patients (plus 404). Phone, address, biography, credentials, and condition content remain unconfirmed. A successful production build does not mean the site is ready for patients. | Keep approval statuses intact. Obtain practice sign-off before launch. |
| Medium | All assets received a year of immutable caching, including the contact card, portrait, and share image whose URLs do not change. | Generate exact cache rules: only fingerprinted CSS, JS, and fonts are immutable; other assets must revalidate. Exact rules avoid overlapping host policies. |
| Medium | Approving a condition and its detail page before biography/training creates a link to a nonexistent About page. | Gate that link on the same content availability used by About. Regression fixture covers independent approvals. |
| Medium | Production guard missed `flag flag-draft` and approved placeholder image references. | Match marker class tokens and placeholder references. Test accidental placeholder approval. |
| Medium | JSON-LD inserted raw JSON into a script element; a future content value containing a script end tag could break out of it. | Escape `<` in embedded JSON and verify parsing with an adversarial fixture. |
| Medium | A custom 404 served at a nested missing URL resolves relative assets/navigation under the missing directory. | Set the 404 base to the site root. Hosting assumes a root-mounted site; subdirectory deployments need a configured base path. |
| Low | Direct telephone links and the mobile directions link omitted analytics event hooks. | Add consistent event attributes. Analytics remains disabled. |
| Medium | CI built pages but did not exercise partial approval or internal link integrity. | Add six isolated, zero-dependency Node tests and run them in CI. |

### Evidence and limits

- Six regression tests pass on Node 22.13.0. Fixtures use temporary directories and never modify the real content record.
- Link, fragment, asset, single-h1, and duplicate-ID checks pass for staging, current production, and selected partial-approval fixtures.
- Staging generates 13 content pages plus 404; current production generates two content pages plus 404.
- Desktop browser walkthrough: homepage → coronary artery disease → Contact. Contact actions and consent-based map button are present; no phone call was initiated.
- Staging homepage compressed first-load build estimate: 91.9 KB. This is a size estimate, not a new Lighthouse measurement.
- This pass did not repeat the prior mobile/axe/Lighthouse audit or validate live analytics, external maps, hosting response headers, or the production custom-404 routing. Python's preview server does not apply `_headers` or custom 404 routing.
- The default Homebrew Node 22 binary fails to load its simdjson library. The existing `/Users/todrod/.local/node/bin/node` works; no global installation was changed.

### Next work, in order

1. Confirm the practice identity, current phone/address, office hours, referral process, and approved headshot.
2. Review the draft biography, condition pages, and visit instructions with the practice; approve or edit each field individually.
3. Choose the domain and hosting platform, then verify actual cache/security headers and nested 404 handling on that host.
4. Validate mobile navigation, keyboard use, and accessibility again after the approved content and portrait are in place.
5. If analytics is selected, verify provider-specific script/event/CSP behavior before enabling it. Existing provider integration has not been exercised in this pass.

## Final prelaunch pass — November 2026

**Status: local technical checks pass; full public launch still requires content and hosting setup.**

### Verified and fixed

- Seven Node tests pass, including staging and production local links, fragment IDs,
  asset existence, unique IDs, one H1, production content filtering, structured-data
  escaping, cache rules, and controllable MP4 heart markup on Home and About.
- Browser smoke checks covered all 14 staging routes at 375px: no horizontal page
  overflow, unnamed buttons, or images missing alt attributes. No console errors
  were captured during that route sweep. This is not a full WCAG conformance audit.
- About previously rendered a static heart poster. It now uses the same lightweight
  looping MP4 as Home, with verified pause/resume. Reduced-motion and offscreen/tab
  visibility handling remain in place. The header logo intentionally runs only two
  introductory beats; it is separate from the anatomical heart animation.
- Added a copper-lit navy background behind the About introduction and a bordered
  portrait/name treatment, plus a warm metallic wash behind the shared header brand.
- Corrected the build size report: approximately 96 KiB is HTML/CSS/JS/fonts only,
  not total page download. The poster is 43 KiB, logo 11 KiB, and MP4 141 KiB.
  The 1.73 MiB GIF is not referenced by pages. Condition images are 34–50 KiB each
  and lazy-loaded below the fold. Fonts are self-hosted; no framework runtime.
